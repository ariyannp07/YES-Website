# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

The primary users are Yale students deciding whether to build, current student builders looking for collaborators and peers, alumni looking to reconnect with Yale’s builder community, and visitors evaluating what YES actually does. Most arrive on a phone between other commitments and need to understand the organization, find credible people or work, and identify a way in without decoding internal club language.

## Product Purpose

The site makes the Yale Entrepreneurial Society legible and useful. It should establish why YES exists, prove that meaningful building is already happening, help visitors discover people and press coverage, and make participation feel concrete. Success means a first-time visitor can understand YES within one screen, reach a relevant person or piece of work within two actions, and distinguish public opportunities from initiatives that are not yet open.

## Brand Personality

Ambitious, grounded, communal. The voice is direct and opinionated without sounding self-important; proof and artifacts carry more weight than promotion. The experience should feel like entering an active room of people making things, not visiting an institutional brochure or a venture-capital portfolio.

## Anti-references

The current site is the primary anti-reference where spectacle precedes substance: a long globe sequence hides navigation, internal labels make useful content hard to predict, and the globe, blueprint, catalog, and text pages feel like separate microsites. Avoid generic startup gradients, anonymous “innovation” imagery, empty institutional prestige, ornate editorial affectation, and interfaces that make users wait before they can read or act.

## Design Principles

1. Lead with evidence. Use the Wall Street Journal story, real builders, and specific work as the first proof of relevance.
2. Make the way in obvious. Thesis, People, Common Room, the homepage Press section, and Join should read as a coherent public structure.
3. Let identity support access. Motion and graphic systems may create atmosphere, but navigation, copy, and content remain immediately available.
4. Show the network. People with consented images lead the directory; monograms remain deliberate and honest rather than pretending imagery exists.
5. State unfinished work plainly. Pending programs and disconnected integrations are labeled directly with no false urgency or implied availability.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Maintain keyboard-operable navigation and forms, visible focus states, semantic headings and landmarks, 4.5:1 minimum body-text contrast, reduced-motion alternatives, and layouts that remain usable at 320px width and 200% zoom. Never make canvas, animation, color, or hover the only carrier of meaning.
